# Grant Writing

A force multiplier for the science, not a substitute for it. The ideas are the applicant's;
this skill keeps the narrative fundable and the submission compliant.

## First step: load the call's guidelines

Do not draft until the specific call's rules are on the table. Funders reject on format and
misfit before they ever judge the science.

1. Check [guidelines/](guidelines/) for a file matching this call (see
   [guidelines/README.md](guidelines/README.md) for the convention). If one exists, read it.
2. If none exists, ask the applicant for the call text or link, or extract the rules from a
   document they provide.
3. Produce a short reminder checklist before writing a word: eligibility, page or character
   limits per section, the required sections and their order, the evaluation criteria and
   their weights, budget rules, formatting requirements, and the deadline.

Completion criterion: the applicant has seen the reminder checklist for this specific call and
confirmed it, or has explicitly said to proceed without one. Only then start drafting.

## The narrative spine

The full spine with drafting prompts is in
[references/narrative-spine.md](references/narrative-spine.md). The shape:

- **Problem.** Why this matters and why now. Concrete, not a literature tour.
- **Gap.** The specific thing that is missing or unknown. One sentence a reviewer can repeat.
- **Approach.** The idea, and why you and why now. This is the pitch.
- **Aims.** Testable, sized to the money and time, and independent. Aim 2 must not collapse if
  Aim 1 fails. Dominoed aims are the most common structural flaw.
- **Impact.** What changes if this works, for the field and beyond it.
- **Feasibility and risk.** Preliminary data, track record, environment, and an honest
  contingency for each real risk.

## The reviewer's-eye pass

Reviewers are expert, overloaded, and skimming. After drafting, read the application as one of
them would.

- Can the ask, the novelty, and the feasibility be found in under a minute of skimming? If they
  are buried, move them up and set them off.
- Is every claim either cited or shown with data? Unsupported assertions read as hope.
- Is there one figure that carries the central idea on its own?
- Does the text map explicitly onto the call's evaluation criteria, in the funder's own terms?
  Reviewers often score against a rubric; make their job mechanical.

## Common rejection reasons, and how to preempt them

- **Over-ambitious for the budget and time.** Scope the aims to what the money buys. Reviewers
  punish plans they do not believe.
- **Contingent aims.** Restructure so each aim stands alone.
- **No clear gap.** State the gap in one sentence and return to it.
- **Feasibility not shown.** Preliminary data, prior track record, named collaborators and
  letters, and the infrastructure you already have.
- **Misfit to the scheme.** Address the call's stated priorities directly. A strong proposal to
  the wrong call still loses.
- **Jargon wall.** Assume a smart reviewer from an adjacent field. Define the essential terms
  once and move on.

## Budget and work plan

The work plan and Gantt should map one to one onto the aims. Every cost is justified against a
specific piece of the work. A timeline that does not match the aims signals a plan that was not
thought through.

## Style

Run the prose through the `deslop` skill. American English by default. If the funder mandates
British spelling or a house style, follow the funder; that overrides the lab default, and it is
the one place to deviate. Cut ruthlessly to the limit; a tight application reads as competent.
